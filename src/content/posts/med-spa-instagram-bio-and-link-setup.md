A prospect who lands on your Instagram profile gives it about five seconds. In that time they decide whether you are a real clinic, whether you do the thing they want, whether you are near them, and whether booking looks easy. Most med spa profiles fail at least one of those, usually the last one.

This guide sets the profile up so it passes all four: the name and bio, the link setup, highlights, and the action button. It is part of our [complete guide to Instagram marketing for med spas](/posts/instagram-marketing-for-med-spas/).

## First: make it a business account

Everything else in this guide depends on having a business account. Meta's setup page lists what it gives you: insights to "track your account performance using real-time data and learn what's working well with your audience", the ability to share contact information on your profile, and running ads from the account [3]. Switching takes a minute: open your profile settings, go to *Account type and tools*, choose *Switch to professional account*, pick the category that fits your clinic, and select *Business* [3].

If your account is still personal, do this before anything else.

## The name field is a search field

Your username is your handle. The *name* field, the bold line above the bio, is separate and searchable inside Instagram. Use it.

- Weak: `Glow Aesthetics`
- Better: `Glow Aesthetics · Med Spa Austin`
- Also good: `Dr. Patel · Injectables & Laser · Denver`

Someone in your city typing "med spa" into Instagram search is more likely to find the second and third versions. Put the clinic name first so it still reads naturally.

## The bio: 150 characters, four facts

You have 150 characters [1]. Prospects are scanning for facts, so give them four:

1. **What you do** — the two or three treatment categories you want to be known for, not a full menu
2. **Who it's for** — if you specialise (skin of colour, men, first-timers, over 50), say so
3. **Where you are** — city or neighbourhood
4. **How to book** — a pointer to the link or button

Examples that work:

> Injectables, laser & medical-grade skincare · Austin, TX
> Nurse-led, no pressure consults
> Book below ↓

> Results-first aesthetics for first-timers · Denver
> Botox · Filler · Microneedling
> Free consult — tap Book

What to leave out: slogans ("Confidence starts here"), hashtags, and emoji strings that push the facts off the screen. If a line doesn't help someone decide to book, cut it.

## Links: one destination, or a short menu

Instagram lets you put up to five links directly on your profile; the feature launched in April 2023 [2]. You add them by tapping *Edit profile*, then *Links*, then *Add external link*, and you can drag them into the order you want [2]. Only the first link is visible without a tap; the rest show when a visitor expands the list.

For a med spa, the right setup is usually one of these:

**Option A — one link, straight to booking.** Best when almost every visitor wants the same thing. The link should open your scheduler's consult page, not your homepage. Test it on a phone: if it takes more than two taps to reach a calendar, fix the destination.

**Option B — booking first, then a short menu.** Booking link in slot one, then two or three more: a treatment menu with pricing, a new-client offer, a page for your membership or financing. Keep it to three or four; five identical-looking links slow people down.

**Option C — a link page.** If you run promotions that change weekly or want to track which link people tap, a simple link page (yours or a link-in-bio tool) in slot one works — as long as the first button on that page is *Book a consult*.

Whichever you pick, name the link clearly. "Book a consult" beats "Website".

## The action button: booking without leaving the app

Business accounts can add an action button to the profile — the button that sits next to *Message* and lets someone book without ever opening a browser. You add it from *Edit profile*, then under *Public business information* tap *Action buttons*, pick the button you want, and connect it to your online service [4]. You need an existing account with one of Instagram's supported partners to connect it, and only a limited set of providers is available [4]; check whether your scheduling software is on the list. One button shows on the profile at a time [4].

If your scheduler is supported, set this up. It removes the single biggest source of drop-off between "interested" and "booked": leaving Instagram.

## Highlights: the tour a prospect takes before they scroll

Highlights sit right under the bio and are tapped before the grid is read. Treat them as a five-screen tour of the clinic:

- **Results** — with written client consent, factual captions, and within your state's rules for advertising medical results
- **Treatments** — one Story per service: what it is, who it's for, downtime, starting price
- **The team** — faces, credentials, what each person does
- **The space** — reception, treatment rooms, parking
- **FAQ / Pricing** — the questions your front desk answers all day

Use matching covers, short titles, and update them quarterly. An "Offers" highlight showing a promotion from last year tells a prospect nobody is home.

## Contact details and category

Check the small things on the business profile: the category reads *Medical Spa* or the closest available option; the address is filled in so the map pin works; the phone and email are the ones your front desk actually monitors. Meta's setup page describes sharing contact information as one of the core reasons to use a business account [3] — make sure what you share is current.

## A five-second test

Hand your phone to someone who has never seen the profile and ask them, after five seconds, to answer four questions: What does this clinic do? Where is it? Would I trust them? How do I book? If any answer is a shrug, the profile isn't finished yet.

Then check the booking path yourself once a month. Links break, schedulers change URLs, and action buttons disconnect. A profile that looks perfect and leads to a dead page is the most expensive kind of mistake, because the content already did its job.
