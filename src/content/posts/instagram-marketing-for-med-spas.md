Instagram is where a prospective client looks you up the night before they decide whether to book a consultation. That is the whole reason a med spa should care about it. Not follower counts, not going viral — the moment someone in your area is deciding between you and the clinic two miles away, and your profile is one of the things they use to decide.

This guide covers the full job: setting the account up properly, deciding what to post and how often, choosing between Reels and carousels, and measuring whether any of it is turning into bookings. Each section links to a deeper guide where the detail matters.

## Why Instagram matters for a med spa specifically

Aesthetic treatments are considered purchases. People research them, compare providers, and want to see real results before they trust someone with their face. Instagram is built for exactly that kind of looking.

The numbers back it up. Sprout Social reports that 60% of consumers interact with brand content on Instagram at least multiple times a week, and that over a quarter of social users from every generation turn to Instagram to find their next purchase [1]. The same report notes that 29% of users make purchases on Instagram itself [1]. For a med spa, the purchase happens in your booking system, not in the app — but the decision often starts on your profile.

Two things follow from that:

1. **Your profile is a landing page.** Bio, highlights, pinned posts, and the link are the first screen a prospect sees. Most accounts treat them as an afterthought.
2. **Consistency beats intensity.** A prospect who finds you today needs to see an account that looked alive last week and the week before, not a burst of posts from a promotion three months ago followed by silence.

## Step 1: Set the account up like a business

Before content, get the structure right. Switch the account to a **business account** if it isn't one already. Meta's own setup page lists what that unlocks: insights ("track your account performance using real-time data and learn what's working well with your audience"), the ability to share contact information on the profile, and running ads directly from the account [6]. The switch is a few taps: profile settings, then *Account type and tools*, then *Switch to professional account*, pick a category, and choose *Business* [6].

Then fix the profile itself:

- **Name field**: include what you do and where. "Glow Aesthetics · Med Spa · Austin" is searchable inside Instagram in a way "Glow Aesthetics" alone is not.
- **Bio**: 150 characters. Say who you treat, what you're known for, and how to book. Skip the inspirational quote.
- **Link**: one booking destination, or a short link page if you need several. Make sure it opens the booking flow, not your homepage.
- **Highlights**: results (with consent), the team, the space, pricing or membership, FAQs. Prospects tap these before they scroll your grid.
- **Action button**: a *Book now* button connected to your scheduling provider, if yours is supported.

Our guide to [bio and link setup](/posts/med-spa-instagram-bio-and-link-setup/) walks through each of these with examples.

## Step 2: Decide what you are actually posting

Most med spa accounts drift into one of two failure modes: an endless stream of promotional graphics nobody engages with, or sporadic bursts of content whenever someone on the team remembers. The fix is a small set of recurring content types you can produce every week without a photoshoot.

A workable mix for a clinic:

| Content type | What it does | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment explainers | Answers the questions people ask at consults: what it is, who it's for, what it feels like, downtime | Weekly |
| Results (with consent) | The single most persuasive thing you can post | Weekly or as available |
| Team and space | Builds the trust that a stranger needs before letting you near their face | Every couple of weeks |
| Client questions answered | Take real DMs and consult questions and answer them on camera | Weekly |
| Offers and availability | Openings this week, seasonal packages, membership | Only when true |

Notice that "offers" is the smallest slice. Accounts that post only promotions train followers to scroll past them.

Two content rules that matter more in aesthetics than in most industries:

- **Consent and compliance are not optional.** Before-and-after content needs written client consent, and advertising rules for medical results vary by state and licensing body. Keep captions factual and avoid promising outcomes.
- **Real people outperform stock.** A ten-second phone video of your injector explaining what "tox" actually does will beat a designed graphic every time. Prospects are trying to decide whether they trust the person, and a graphic tells them nothing.

## Step 3: Post often enough, and no more than you can sustain

The question every owner asks is "how often." The honest answer from the data is: less than you fear, more than most clinics manage.

Hootsuite's 2026 benchmark research found that across industries the highest engagement rate (4.04%) came from accounts posting two feed posts per week, and for healthcare specifically the peak (3.89%) was also at two posts per week [3]. Their earlier healthcare-specific research put it plainly: post at least twice a week, and expect diminishing returns if you post too frequently [7].

For growth (as opposed to engagement), Instagram's own creator FAQ says the accounts with the highest net follower growth post ten or more Reels a month on average [8] — that's roughly two to three a week. So a realistic target for a clinic is **two to four feed posts a week, at least half of them Reels, plus Stories most days**. What matters far more than the exact number is that it holds for months. Read the full breakdown in [how often a med spa should post on Instagram](/posts/how-often-should-a-med-spa-post-on-instagram/).

## Step 4: Pick formats for the job they do

Reels and carousels do different things, and the data is unusually clear about which.

Buffer's analysis of more than four million posts found that Reels get 1.36 times the reach of carousels and 2.25 times the reach of single-image posts, while carousels get 1.12 times the engagement of Reels and 2.14 times the engagement of single images [5]. Socialinsider's 2025 study of 35 million posts tells the same story from the engagement side: carousels averaged a 0.55% engagement rate, Reels 0.52%, and static images 0.37% — with image engagement down 17% year over year [2].

So the rule for a clinic is simple:

- **Reels to reach people who don't follow you yet.** Treatment explainers, "what to expect" walkthroughs, a day in the clinic.
- **Carousels to convince people who are already looking.** Before-and-after series (with consent), step-by-step treatment guides, pricing and package breakdowns, FAQ slides.
- **Single images sparingly**, and only when the image is the point — a finished result, a new piece of equipment, the team.

Instagram's own explanation of how ranking works fits this. For Feed and Stories, it predicts how likely you are to spend a few seconds on a post, comment, like, reshare, and tap the profile photo; for Reels, how likely you are to watch it all the way through, like it, find it entertaining, and go to the audio page [4]. A Reel that a stranger watches to the end and a carousel that a prospect saves are both exactly what the system is looking for. Our [Reels vs carousels guide](/posts/med-spa-instagram-reels-vs-carousels/) goes into what to make in each format.

## Step 5: Run Stories like a front desk

Stories don't build your grid, but they keep you visible to the people who already follow you — the ones most likely to book. Use them the way a good front desk uses a conversation:

- Openings and cancellations this week
- Quick answers to a question someone DMed
- A client walking out happy (with permission)
- Polls and question boxes that give you content ideas for next week

A few Stories most days is enough. They should feel unpolished on purpose.

## Step 6: Turn attention into bookings

Reach without a path to booking is just entertainment. Check each of these once a quarter:

- **Can someone book in two taps from your profile?** Link goes to the booking page; action button is set up; the booking page works on a phone.
- **Are you answering DMs?** Prospects ask about pricing and availability in DMs before they book. Slow or missing replies lose bookings that the content already earned.
- **Do your captions tell people what to do next?** "DM us 'lips' for pricing" or "link in bio to book a consult" — one clear next step per post, not three.
- **Are your Highlights current?** An "Offers" highlight showing last year's holiday package signals nobody is home.

## Step 7: Measure what actually matters

Follower count is the least useful number on the screen. Look at these instead, monthly:

1. **Reach among non-followers** (from Reels insights). Are new local people seeing you?
2. **Engagement rate per post** — engagements divided by followers, tracked against your own past months. Benchmarks vary widely by method: Socialinsider's cross-industry average is 0.48% per post and fell 24% year over year in 2025 [2], while Hootsuite reports healthcare peaks near 3.89% using a different calculation [3]. Pick one method and watch the trend.
3. **Profile visits and link taps** — the step between seeing you and booking.
4. **Days since your last post.** If it's more than seven, nothing else on this list will improve.
5. **Consults that mention Instagram.** Ask at intake. It's the only number that connects the account to revenue.

If engagement is falling while reach is flat, the content is the problem. If reach is falling, cadence or format is. If both are fine but bookings aren't moving, the profile-to-booking path is broken. Each has a different fix, and the numbers tell you which one to make.

## What a healthy med spa account looks like

Put together, the account that books consults from Instagram looks like this: a business account with a clear name, a specific bio, and a working booking link; two to four posts a week that hold steady for months; Reels for reach and carousels for persuasion; Stories that feel like a conversation; DMs answered the same day; and a monthly look at reach, engagement, and consults that mention Instagram.

None of it requires a big following, an agency, or a content studio. It requires a system that the team can keep running on an ordinary Tuesday.
