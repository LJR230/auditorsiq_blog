Every med spa owner eventually asks the same question: how often do we actually need to post? The answer from the data is clearer than the advice floating around suggests, and it is less demanding than most owners fear. What the data punishes is not posting *too little*; it's posting *unevenly*.

This guide covers what the published benchmarks say, what Instagram itself recommends, and a weekly cadence a clinic with a small team can keep running for months. It is part of our [complete guide to Instagram marketing for med spas](/posts/instagram-marketing-for-med-spas/).

## What the benchmarks say

Two sets of figures are worth knowing, because they measure different things.

**Engagement peaks at a modest cadence.** Hootsuite's 2026 benchmark research found that across industries the highest engagement rate, 4.04%, came from accounts posting two feed posts per week. For healthcare specifically, the peak engagement rate of 3.89% was also at two posts per week [2]. Their healthcare-specific research from the year before reached the same conclusion in plainer language: healthcare accounts are most likely to keep their audiences engaged by posting at least twice per week, and you start to see diminishing returns if you post too frequently [1].

That doesn't mean two is a ceiling. It means that beyond a couple of posts a week, each additional post earns less engagement per post — which is fine if the extra posts are still good, and a problem if they're filler.

**Growth rewards a higher Reels count.** Instagram's own FAQ for creators says that when they look at the accounts with the greatest net follower growth, those accounts post, on average, ten or more Reels per month [3]. That's roughly two to three a week. It's a growth signal, not an engagement one: Reels are how you reach people who don't follow you yet.

Put the two together and a picture emerges. Two to four feed posts a week, with Reels making up at least half of them, sits in the range where engagement is still strong and growth is possible. Fewer than two and you're invisible; more than four and quality usually slips for a clinic team.

## What is actually happening at other clinics

It helps to know the baseline. Socialinsider's 2025 study of 35 million posts from more than 447,000 business pages found that brands posted 33% more Reels than the year before, while static image posts fell from about ten a month to about seven [4]. In other words, the average business account posts several times a week, and the mix is shifting toward video.

Most med spa accounts we review are below that. The common pattern is a burst of activity around an opening or a promotion, then long gaps. The engagement numbers from those accounts are not bad because the content is bad; they are bad because the account goes quiet, followers stop expecting it, and each new post starts cold.

## Why consistency matters more than the exact number

Instagram decides how far a post travels by watching how the first people who see it react. That means every post is an audition in front of your existing followers, and their response depends heavily on whether they're used to hearing from you.

An account posting twice a week, every week, has followers who recognise it in the feed and respond to it. An account that posts nine times in a week and then nothing for three weeks trains those same followers to ignore it. The second account may have the same monthly count; it will not get the same result.

This is the single most useful reframing for a clinic: **the goal is a cadence you will still be hitting in six months**, not the highest number you can manage this week.

## A weekly cadence a clinic can sustain

Here is a schedule built around a two-person team spending two to three hours a week on Instagram, with no photoshoots.

**Feed (three posts a week)**

- **One Reel:** a 15–30 second treatment explainer or "what to expect" walkthrough, filmed on a phone by your injector or aesthetician. Same format every week; only the treatment changes.
- **One carousel:** results with client consent, a step-by-step treatment guide, a package breakdown, or answers to the three questions you got asked most this week.
- **One flexible slot:** a second Reel when you have a good one, otherwise a team or clinic post. This is the post you drop if the week gets busy.

**Stories (most days)**

- Openings and cancellations
- A quick answer to a DM question
- A client leaving happy, with permission
- A poll or question box once a week — this fills next week's content ideas

**Batching**

Film Reels in one thirty-minute block every other week: four to six takes, one setup, everyone in scrubs. Write carousel copy in one sitting. Schedule the feed posts in advance and leave Stories live.

If three feed posts a week is too many right now, do two and hold it. Do not do four this week and zero next week.

## When to post more, and when to post less

**Post more when:**

- You have a real backlog of consented results and genuine questions to answer
- A Reel is clearly reaching non-followers and you can make more like it
- A new treatment or a seasonal package needs explaining from several angles

**Post less when:**

- Posts are becoming graphics with text on them because nobody had time to film
- Engagement per post is falling as your count rises — the diminishing-returns signal Hootsuite describes [1]
- The person doing it is burning out. A cadence that depends on one exhausted person is not a cadence.

## How to check whether your cadence is working

Look at three numbers once a month:

1. **Days since your last feed post.** If it's ever more than seven, nothing else matters yet.
2. **Posts per week over the last eight weeks.** Not this week — the trend. Are you steady, or bursting and stopping?
3. **Engagement per post, compared with your own previous months.** Benchmarks like Hootsuite's 3.89% for healthcare [2] are useful for direction, but different sources calculate engagement differently, so your own trend is the number that tells you whether the cadence is right.

If the first number is small, the second is steady, and the third is flat or rising, the cadence is right. Move on to what you're posting and how you're converting attention into bookings — the other half of the picture.
