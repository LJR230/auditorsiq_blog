Reels and carousels are not competing for the same job. Reels are how strangers in your area find you; carousels are how the people already looking at you decide to book. Once you see them that way, the "which is better" question dissolves into "which one do I need for this post."

This guide lays out what the data says about each format, how Instagram ranks them, and a specific list of what a med spa should make in each. It is part of our [complete guide to Instagram marketing for med spas](/posts/instagram-marketing-for-med-spas/).

## The data: Reels win reach, carousels win engagement

The clearest evidence comes from Buffer's analysis of more than four million Instagram posts published through its platform between January 2022 and October 2024. The findings [1]:

- Reels got **1.36 times the reach** of carousels and **2.25 times the reach** of single-photo posts.
- Carousels got **1.12 times the engagement** of Reels and **2.14 times the engagement** of single-image posts.

Socialinsider's independent study of 35 million posts across 2025 lines up with that on the engagement side. Carousels averaged a 0.55% engagement rate, Reels 0.52%, and static images 0.37% [2]. The same study found image engagement down 17% year over year, while businesses posted 33% more Reels than the year before [2].

Reels also dominate attention on the platform: Sprout Social, citing Meta data, reports that Reels make up more than half of the daily time people spend on Instagram [5].

So the picture is consistent across sources:

| Format | Best for | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Reels | Reaching people who don't follow you | Slightly lower engagement per view; needs someone on camera |
| Carousels | Engagement, saves, persuading warm prospects | Reaches mostly existing followers |
| Single images | Occasional, when one image is the point | Lowest reach and engagement, and declining |

## Why the formats behave differently

Instagram explained its ranking in a 2021 post that still describes the basic logic. For Feed and Stories, it predicts how likely you are to spend a few seconds on a post, comment on it, like it, reshare it, and tap the profile photo. For Reels, it predicts how likely you are to watch a Reel all the way through, like it, say it was entertaining or funny, and go to the audio page [3].

Two consequences for a clinic:

**Reels are judged on completion.** A 20-second explainer that people watch to the end outperforms a 75-second clinic tour that most people leave. Answer one question per Reel and stop.

**Carousels get a second chance.** Buffer's 2026 breakdown of the algorithm describes how, when a follower doesn't swipe through to the end, Instagram treats the unseen slides as new content and can resurface the carousel later, starting from the first slide they didn't reach [4]. A ten-slide before-and-after series or FAQ carousel effectively gets multiple attempts at the same person. That is a large part of why carousels hold up so well on engagement.

Buffer's algorithm guide also notes that shares — particularly sends via DM — are weighted heavily for Reels distribution [4]. For a med spa, a Reel that one friend sends to another ("this is the place I was telling you about") is the best possible outcome, and it's worth making content people would actually send.

## What a med spa should make as Reels

Reels are for people who have never heard of you. Each one should let a stranger understand one thing about a treatment or your clinic in under 30 seconds, with a real person on screen.

- **Treatment explainers.** "What tox actually does to a muscle, in 20 seconds." "Who is a good candidate for lip filler, and who isn't." One treatment, one question, one answer.
- **What to expect.** The walk from check-in to the treatment room to walking out. Prospects are nervous; showing the ordinary reality removes a reason not to book.
- **Myth corrections.** "Filler doesn't stretch your skin" — the misconceptions your injectors correct at every consult.
- **A day in the clinic.** Short, unpolished, human. The team is the product.
- **Results in motion, with consent.** A slow pan across a healed result, or a client describing how they feel a month later.

Keep the format identical week to week: same framing, same opening line, same length. Prospects start to recognise it, and the team can film six in one session.

## What a med spa should make as carousels

Carousels are for people who are already on your profile and deciding. They should be worth saving.

- **Before-and-after series, with written consent.** Slide one: the result. Then the before, the treatment plan, the number of sessions, realistic timelines. Keep captions factual and within your state's advertising rules for medical results.
- **Step-by-step treatment guides.** "Your first microneedling appointment, slide by slide." These get saved and sent to friends.
- **Pricing and package breakdowns.** People want to know before they DM. A clear carousel saves your front desk twenty messages a week.
- **The questions we got asked this week.** Three to five real questions from DMs and consults, one per slide.
- **Aftercare instructions.** Useful to existing clients, reassuring to prospects.

Design rule: slide one has to work as a single image, because that's all the feed shows until someone swipes.

## When a single image is still right

Post a single image when the image *is* the content and a carousel would be padding: a finished result that needs no context, a new device arriving, the team at an event, a seasonal opening. Given that image engagement is the lowest of the three formats and falling [2], treat it as the exception.

## A simple mix for the week

For a clinic posting three times a week: one Reel for reach, one carousel for persuasion, and one flexible slot that goes to a second Reel when you have a good one. If you post twice, make it one Reel and one carousel. Track two numbers monthly — reach among non-followers (Reels are doing their job) and saves per carousel (carousels are doing theirs) — and shift the mix toward whichever is lagging.
